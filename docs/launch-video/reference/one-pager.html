your desktop sidekick

A little robot that lives on your desk. It listens, has moods, keeps a diary and helps at the computer. Now it helps kids with math too: a hint, a check, or one step, never the whole answer.

what eve does

A robot with ears, hands and a memory

eve is a small robot head with a camera, two motors, 12 lights, a speaker and a touch pad. An app on your Mac gives it ears, hands and a memory. works today

Talks with you

  • Hears “hey eve.” The wake word is heard on the Mac, so nothing is sent anywhere until you speak.
  • Answers on its own screen. Replies appear as captions under its eyes while it beeps and boops.
  • Searches the web. It answers questions about today, like the weather, scores or prices, in one short line.
  • Thinks hard when it has to. Math, code and planning questions go to a slower, stronger model.
  • Remembers your talks. It keeps a few lines about each chat, never the words. Say “remember that” and it keeps the fact for good.
  • Understands “bye” and “be quiet.” Say it however you like: it ends the chat, or goes silent and keeps moving.

Uses your computer

  • Does tasks on your Mac. “Open Spotify and play something.” It clicks and types, up to 25 steps. It asks before it sends, pays or deletes anything. You can change the plan midway or say “stop.”
  • Keeps you company while you code. It naps when Claude Code is idle, looks busy while Claude works, and searches the room for you when a session needs you. Tap it to jump to that window. It wiggles when a task is done.
  • Takes meeting notes. Start it from the Mac and it writes down what's said. Then it lists the decisions, the to-dos and the open questions.

Sees and moves

  • Follows your face. It turns to look at you, learns where you usually sit, and knows you from a stranger.
  • Looks where you ask. “What do you see?” “Look left.” “Where's my mug?” It searches the room and turns to face the mug.
  • Dances. It can sway, bob, nod, wiggle and do a figure-8. For now you start a dance from the Mac, not by voice.
  • Loves a head pat. Pat its head and its lights beat pink and it chirps happily.

Has a life of its own

  • Explores. After 10 quiet minutes, or when you say “go explore,” it looks around the room and notices what's new.
  • Has moods. It gets curious, happy, bored, lonely or startled, and you can see it in its eyes, lights, head and chirps.
  • Keeps a diary. It only writes down thoughts that are new or interesting. At night it sleeps on the day and dreams.
  • Takes photos of cool things. It takes pictures on its own, at most 2 an hour and 8 a day.
  • Lives in a widget. A desktop widget shows its mood and latest thoughts. The diary app has 7 tabs, from Talking to Dreams.
new for kids
who it's for

Two people sit at the desk

the kid

Stuck halfway through a problem

They want help right at the line where they got lost. They don't want a lecture, and they don't want to start over.

the grown-up

Wants the kid to do the thinking

They want a helper that won't do the homework. Every hint, check and step eve gives is small, and eve's work always looks different from the kid's.

the new feature

“Okay eve, I want to do a math lesson”

There are two ways in. Both end up on a whiteboard, where the kid writes and eve waits to be asked for help.

three kinds of help

Small help, one piece at a time

Give me a hint

“What do both sides have in common?”

A nudge in the right direction. eve does not finish a step for the kid.

Check my work

eve reads the board and asks three questions, in this order:

  1. Can I read the writing?
  2. Are the steps right?
  3. Is it finished?

If something is wrong, eve explains the first slip only and asks for another try.

Show one step

“Here's the next line, and why.”

eve writes exactly one step and explains it. Its writing looks different from the kid's. Tap again to see the next step.

The whole answer is never on the menu. The most eve gives at once is one step.

proactive

eve speaks first

A worksheet waits for you. eve starts conversations, both during a lesson and the rest of the time it's on the desk.

In a lesson designed

  • when a problem is on the board“Write your ideas, or show me where you're stuck.”
  • when it can't read a symbol“Is that a 7 or a 1?”
  • when a step is wrong“Look at line two again. Want another go?”
  • when the problem is done“So how did we do that?”

Still to decide

  • Voice or captions. Today eve replies in captions on its screen. Kids who are just learning to read may need to hear it.
  • Where the whiteboard lives. The family computer with a mouse, a tablet with a pen, or both.
  • What stays from the grown-up version. Today eve can use the Mac when you ask it to. A kid's eve may need that switched off.
