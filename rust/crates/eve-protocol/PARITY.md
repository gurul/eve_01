## Boundaries
None. The CJK tables are data generated once from CPython (`tools/gen_codec_tables.py`), not a runtime call.

## Differences
- Codecs: only `utf-8`, `gbk`, `big5` and `shift_jis` (and their CPython aliases) are known. Another codec Python knows (`latin-1`, `cp1252`, ...) is treated as unknown: `sanitize_for_stick` falls back to ASCII and `encode` returns `LookupError` on a string. Callers only pass `CJK_CODECS` values.
- `LineAssembler` parses with serde_json, whose `Value` cannot hold what Python's `json.loads` can: a line with `NaN`, `Infinity`, an out-of-range float like `1e999`, a lone `\ud800` escape, or nesting past serde's 128 levels is dropped, and an integer beyond u64 arrives as a lossy float. The stick never sends such lines.
- `truncate_utf8_bytes` takes `usize`; Python's negative `max_bytes` (returning `…`) is not expressible. Callers pass constants.
- `encode` takes a `serde_json::Value` (Python takes a dict whose values may be any `str()`-able object; non-JSON objects go out as their `str()` there).
